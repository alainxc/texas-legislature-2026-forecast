RSLC Launches “2026 Texas Candidate Spotlight” Series, Announces First Round Spotlights

  • August 26th, 2026

Washington, DC — Today, the Republican State Leadership Committee (RSLC) is rolling out its 2026 Texas Candidate Spotlight series, spotlighting the Republican candidates asking Lone Star State voters for their support this fall. With every Texas House seat and 16 of the state’s 31 Senate seats on the ballot this November, Texas Republicans are working to hold the line against a Democratic push for control in Austin. 

This spotlight showcases the range of experience Republicans bring to Austin: a first-generation college graduate turned education policy advocate, a criminal defense attorney, a certified public accountant and international businesswoman, a chemical engineer, and a former television news anchor turned conservative communicator, representing communities stretching from San Antonio’s South Side to the Dallas Metroplex, the Coastal Bend, and the Tarrant County suburbs.

“Texas Republicans have spent the last two sessions proving what conservative governance can deliver, from historic property tax cuts to universal school choice, to investing in energy infrastructure,” said RSLC President Edith Jorge-Tuñón. “This group of Republican candidates is running in districts across the Lone Star State to keep that record going. This November, Texans face a real choice: keep the governing majority that gets things done or hand the reins to a party that hasn’t run anything well in decades. The RSLC is proud to stand behind these candidates as they make the case to voters.”

Jorge Borrego — Texas House District 118

The first in his family to graduate college, Jorge Borrego went on to serve as K-12 Education Policy Director at the Texas Public Policy Foundation, helping drive passage of the state’s school choice law. Now the Republican nominee for the open District 118 seat and endorsed by Governor Abbott, Borrego is running to cut property taxes and expand opportunity for Bexar County families.

Marc LaHood — Texas House District 121

A San Antonio criminal defense attorney first elected in 2024, Marc LaHood serves on the House Judiciary & Civil Jurisprudence and Transportation Committees where he authored legislation creating a permanent state reserve to strengthen the Texas power grid. LaHood is seeking a second term focused on property tax relief and public safety for North Bexar County.

Angie Chen Button — Texas House District 112

A former certified public accountant and Texas Instruments marketing manager, Angie Chen Button has represented House District 112 since 2009. She chairs the House Trade, Workforce, and Economic Development Committee and was named one of Texas Monthly’s best legislators for her work securing child-care funding for working families in Garland, Richardson, and Rowlett.

Denise Villalobos — Texas House District 34

A chemical engineer by trade, Denise Villalobos flipped House District 34 from Democratic control in 2024. Now on the Appropriations and Natural Resources Committees, she has worked to secure state investment in the Coastal Bend’s energy and water infrastructure and is running for a second term to build on that win.

Leigh Wambsganss — Texas Senate District 9

A former television news anchor turned conservative organizer, Leigh Wambsganss founded Southlake Realty and later served as Chief Communications Officer for Patriot Mobile, leading one of the country’s most effective campaigns against Critical Race Theory in schools. After a narrow loss in January’s special election, Wambsganss is running again to reclaim Senate District 9 for Republicans this November.
