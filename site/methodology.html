Methodology

Plain-language description of how the Texas Legislature 2026 Forecast works. Every weight below is read from one configuration file (config/model_config.yaml) and nothing in the processed data is hand-edited.

The short version

For each seat we estimate the expected Democratic-minus-Republican margin from four layers: (1) how the district has voted in recent statewide races on today's lines, (2) who is running, (3) the statewide political environment implied by public polls and a midterm prior, and (4) district-level polls and outside ratings used as capped nudges. We then run 40,000 simulations in which errors are shared statewide, by region, and by Hispanic voter share, with fat tails, to turn margins into probabilities and chamber-control odds.

Layer 1 · District baseline

The Texas Legislative Council publishes statewide election returns aggregated to the current House (PLANH2316) and Senate (PLANS2168) district lines, which avoids any precinct crosswalk. We confirmed with the Council that the 2025 mid-decade redistricting changed only the congressional map, so 2020, 2022 and 2024 results apply directly. A district's lean is its margin minus the statewide margin in the same race, blended across: pres 2024 (40%), sen 2024 (10%), gov 2022 (20%), ag 2022 (15%), pres 2020 (15%). The district's own legislative results in 2022 and 2024 (contested races only, measured against that year's average contested legislative margin) are blended in at 20% after being shrunk halfway toward the statewide lean, because legislative results carry candidate effects.

Layer 2 · Candidates

  • Incumbency: 2.5 points (House) / 2.5 (Senate); first-term incumbents get 50% of that.
  • Open seats get no incumbency bonus and extra simulation variance (+2.0 points s.d.).
  • Prior performance: 40% of a returning candidate's past over- or under-performance against the district's expectation carries forward (capped at ±12 points before scaling). A non-incumbent who was the party's nominee here before gets 0.75 points. These are our proxies for candidate quality.
  • Campaign finance: Texas Ethics Commission cover-sheet totals of contributions reported since January 2025, with a $25,000 floor added to both sides to suppress noise; effect = 1.5 × log(D/R), capped at ±4.0 points. Candidates with no matched filing are treated as raising only the floor.
  • Uncontested races (one name on the ballot) are called with certainty; write-ins are ignored.
  • Third-party and independent candidates are modelled explicitly. When one major party is absent, the minor candidate is assumed to capture 50% of the absent party's baseline voters plus a 2.5-point base, with extra variance (6.0 points s.d.), so such seats are never simply marked unopposed. When both major parties run, minor candidates do not change the D−R margin.

Layer 3 · Statewide environment

There is no Texas legislative generic ballot poll. Instead the expected statewide margin for a generic legislative race is built from five indicators, each converted to an implied Texas generic margin: Texas generic/congressional-preference questions (weight 0.3); the Texas U.S. Senate race (weight 0.25, adjusted -1.5 points for the Republican nominee's past under-performance of the ticket); the governor race (weight 0.25, adjusted +3.0 to strip the incumbent governor's personal edge); the national generic ballot plus Texas's -11.0 lean versus the nation (weight 0.15); and Abbott net approval (weight 0.05). Each poll is weighted by recency (half-life 21 days), sample size (square root, capped at 1,500), pollster quality (the last public FiveThirtyEight pollster ratings) and partisanship (sponsored or internal polls get 50% weight and are shifted 2.5 points away from the sponsor's side). Polls older than 120 days are dropped.

The poll-based margin is then combined with a structural midterm prior: the 2024 presidential margin shifted 4.0 points toward the party out of the White House, with weight 0.25 when polling is plentiful. Because Texas polls overstated Democrats by about 3.5 points in both backtest years (below), a correction of -2.0 points is applied to the 2026 poll-based margin; the backtests do not use this correction. Current values: poll-based margin R+2.4 (raw R+0.4), prior R+9.7, expected statewide margin R+4.2.

Layer 4 · District polls and outside ratings

Public district-level polls are averaged with the same recency weighting; internal polls get 50% weight and are shifted 3.0 points against the sponsor. The poll average replaces at most 55% of the model's view. Outside ratings (State Navigate, CNalysis, Sabato's Crystal Ball, Ballotpedia battleground lists, and DLCC/RSLC/caucus target lists) are converted to implied margins (Safe ±16, Likely ±9, Lean ±4.5, Tilt ±2, Toss-up 0) and blended in as capped priors: total ratings weight is capped at 30% and the shift at 5.0 points, so ratings can nudge but never dictate a seat. Target lists only pull a seat slightly toward toss-up and add variance.

Simulation

40,000 Monte Carlo runs. Each run draws a statewide error from a Student-t distribution (4 degrees of freedom, scaled to s.d. 4.0 points) so large polling misses are possible; a regional error (s.d. 2.5) shared by seats in the same part of Texas; a Hispanic-share factor (s.d. 3.0, scaled by each district's Spanish-surname voter registration share) capturing the correlated shifts seen in 2020 and 2024; and an independent district error (s.d. 5.5 House / 5.0 Senate, larger for open seats, seats with no recent contested result, and one-major-party races). Senate seats not on the ballot are fixed to their current holders.

Per-seat win probabilities and 80% margin intervals, seat distributions, chamber-control probabilities, tipping-point frequencies, a flip list, and the sensitivity table below.

Backtest

The same pipeline was run for the 2022 and 2024 Texas legislative elections using only statewide results from earlier cycles, that year's candidates and incumbency, and that year's pre-election polls. Not available historically and therefore excluded: campaign finance (no historical pull), external ratings / district polls (not archived). Brier scores are for contested two-party races (0 = perfect, 0.25 = coin flip). The positive margin bias in both years is the pro-Democratic polling error that motivates the correction above.

How this differs from State Navigate and other outlets

State Navigate, CNalysis and Sabato publish categorical ratings informed by expert judgement; the DLCC and RSLC publish target lists that reflect spending priorities. This site publishes probabilities and margin ranges from a transparent, reproducible model, uses their ratings only as capped nudges, models minor-party-only races explicitly, applies a documented Texas polling-bias correction, and backtests itself. It has no access to private polling or field reports, which is where expert raters often have an edge.
